Bite: Food Tracker

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 26 October 2026

In short:

  • Bite has no account and no servers of its own. I don't receive your diary, your weight, your photos or anything else you log.
  • Your data stays on your devices. It also goes to Apple Health if you allow it, and to your own iCloud account if you turn on iCloud sync.
  • Food searches, barcode lookups and recipe links go over the internet. They send only the search words, the barcode or the web address.
  • No ads, no tracking, no analytics of its own and no selling of data.

Who is responsible

Bite is made by Yarne Savaete, an independent developer in Belgium. For questions about this policy or your data, write to yarne.savaete@outlook.com.

What stays on your device

Everything you log in Bite is stored in the app on your iPhone, iPad or Apple Watch. That includes your food diary, recipes, goals, weight and body measurements, water, fasts, supplements, check-ins and symptoms, medication doses, pregnancy mode, prices and receipts, and your meal and progress photos. Progress photos can be locked with Face ID; Bite never sees your face or fingerprint data, only whether unlocking worked.

Photos you take or choose (barcodes, nutrition labels, receipts, recipes, restaurant menus, meals) are read on the device with Apple's text and image recognition. Features that use Apple Intelligence, like describing a meal, recognising the food on a plate, restaurant menus and "Ask your data", use the model on your device.

Like all app data, Bite's data is included in your device's own iCloud or computer backup, if you make those. Bite also keeps a few automatic backups inside the app, on the device. When you delete the app, its data on the device is deleted with it.

What goes over the internet

Bite contacts these services only when you use the feature, and sends only what the feature needs:

  • Open Food Facts (a non-profit food database, privacy policy): your search words, the barcodes you scan, the words read from a product pack, and your language and country so results fit where you live. This is used for search, barcode scanning, comparing products, Visual Intelligence and matching receipts and recipes to foods.
  • USDA FoodData Central (the US Department of Agriculture): a barcode that Open Food Facts doesn't know.
  • Recipe websites: when you import a recipe from a link, Bite loads that web page and its photo, the way a browser would.

These requests never include your diary, your profile or an identifier for you. Like any website you visit, these services can see your device's IP address. Product photos are loaded from Open Food Facts too.

Apple services

Some features work through Apple. What Apple does with that data is covered by Apple's privacy policy, not by me.

  • Apple Health, only if you allow it: Bite saves the food, water, body measurements and workouts you log, and reads the types you choose, like steps, active energy, workouts, sleep, body measurements, blood sugar, blood pressure, cycle tracking and medication doses. Steps, workouts, sleep and body measurements read from Apple Health stay on the device that read them. Blood sugar, blood pressure, cycle days and medication doses are read when they are shown and not stored by Bite at all. Food history you choose to import from Apple Health shows in your diary on the device that imported it and never goes to iCloud: while iCloud sync is on, it isn't imported, and food imported earlier is set aside on that device until you turn sync off.
  • iCloud sync, only if you turn it on: your Bite data syncs between your own devices through your private iCloud account. Measurements, workouts, activity and food imported from Apple Health are left out. Measurements and workouts reach your other devices through Apple Health. I have no access to your iCloud data.
  • Sharing with a partner, only if you set it up: the shopping list and the week plans you choose to share are shared through iCloud with the person you invite. Your diary, goals and weight are never shared.
  • Bite Premium: subscriptions are bought and managed through the App Store. Apple handles payment; I only get sales reports from Apple that don't say who you are.
  • Siri, Shortcuts, Spotlight, widgets, Reminders and the Photos library: Bite uses them when you do, to log by voice, search your foods and recipes, add your shopping list to Reminders or save an image.
  • Crash reports: if you agreed to share analytics with app developers in your device's settings, Apple may send me anonymous crash reports and usage statistics.

What you share yourself

When you share a recipe, a backup, a day or a year in review card, it goes where you send it: AirDrop, Messages, Files or another app. Shared recipe files contain the recipe and its nutrition, not your diary.

Health information

Bite handles information about your health, like what you eat, your weight, symptoms, pregnancy and medication. It stays under your control in the places described above. Bite doesn't use it for advertising, doesn't sell it and doesn't share it with anyone. Bite gives estimates and general information, not medical advice.

Children

Bite is not meant for children under 16 and doesn't knowingly handle their data.

Your rights

Because I don't receive or store your data, there is nothing about you on my side to look up, correct or delete. You can see, change, export (Stats → CSV export, or a backup) and delete all your data in the app itself. To remove the data from Apple Health or iCloud, use the Health app or your iCloud settings.

If you live in the European Union, you can also file a complaint with your data protection authority; in Belgium that is the Data Protection Authority.

Changes

If Bite starts handling data differently, this page is updated first, with a new date at the top.
